# ShieldMate SME — interface usability study

English participant sheet · academic study · synthetic scenario only

## Invitation

We invite SME owners, operations leads and IT generalists who genuinely help make security decisions for small businesses to try ShieldMate's interface for an academic project. You will use a fictional company and fixed sample data, explain what you understand, and give feedback. This is a usability session, not a security assessment or a sales call. Participation is voluntary. No company systems, passwords or confidential information are needed. The team will arrange the session with you; this document is an invitation template, not evidence that anyone has been contacted.

## Consent explanation — read before deciding

The moderator will show the interface, ask one timed question, then ask you to inspect evidence, example work and a sample PDF, and answer six short questions. Session length depends on your pace; agree availability with the moderator. The timed question uses a 120-second research criterion. It tests the interface, not your professional competence. You may skip a question, pause or stop without giving a reason or losing any benefit. A stopped/paused timed trial is recorded honestly; restarting will not replace the original result.

You need not give a name, employer, email, phone number, password or real domain. The team uses a code and broad role/language categories. With core consent, the moderator records actions, task outcomes, observed time, ratings and brief study notes. Exact quotations require separate permission; otherwise notes paraphrase your feedback. Screenshots and audio each require their own optional permission. Refusing any media permission does not prevent participation. Do not enter personal or company secrets anywhere.

Before consent, the moderator must explain who controls the local study files, the exact retention/deletion date, how you can request withdrawal using your code, and who may see a redacted academic appendix. If these details are not completed, the session must not start. Raw evidence stays on the team's access-controlled local machine, excluded from Git; it is not uploaded or published automatically. Only team-reviewed, consented, redacted proof may be selected for the assignment. You may withdraw your responses/media. The team will remove them according to the agreed procedure and, where permitted, retain only a non-identifying enrollment/withdrawal count so exclusions remain transparent. No app telemetry or third-party analytics is added for this study.

Record separately, without pressure: **core study notes yes/no; screenshots yes/no; audio yes/no; verbatim quotations yes/no**. Ask for your participant code if you want to withdraw later. No identifying signature is required by this kit; follow any applicable course/institution procedure.

## Standard opening (moderator reads exactly)

“This is a fictional, read-only example. Nothing here scans your organization or changes a company account. Please use the interface to form your own answer. We are testing how clearly the product communicates, not testing you. You may say that something is unclear. I cannot guide you to the answer during the first task.”

The moderator opens a fresh `/demo` page in your chosen language, starting on Sample assets. The fixed scenario date is illustrative; it is not today's security assessment. No account or real asset is required.

## Tasks

1. **Timed primary task.** “Using this example, find the issue you think should be addressed first. Tell me which issue it is and what you would do next.” Timing starts when this prompt is first shown or read aloud and ends when you submit your answer. Please do not provide confidential examples from your workplace.
2. **After the first answer is recorded**, find the technical evidence supporting your choice and show where you found it.
3. Open the example-work step. Explain what the displayed task status means and whether it proves the underlying technical issue is resolved. This public story is read-only: there is no task-edit button or persistence claim here.
4. Open the sample-report step. Inspect the PDF or ask the moderator to open it with your permission. Explain what it covers, what its dates mean and any limitations you notice. It must be marked DEMO.

You may use larger text, keyboard navigation, screen-reading support or an identical printed/read-aloud prompt. Agree the accessible format before starting; it must not reveal extra clues. The standard criterion stays visible in the research plan; accommodations and interruptions are recorded, not hidden. A separate exploratory follow-up may help understand accessibility needs without replacing the first result.

## Closing questions (optional; no sales claims)

1. How clear were the priorities and next actions? **1 very unclear · 2 unclear · 3 mixed · 4 clear · 5 very clear**
2. How useful could this kind of explanation be in your work? **1 not useful · 2 slightly useful · 3 moderately useful · 4 useful · 5 very useful**. This measures perceived usefulness, not purchase intent.
3. In your own words, how does a task marked Done differ from a finding confirmed Resolved?
4. Where did you hesitate or get stuck, and why?
5. What one change would most improve the experience?
6. Which work decision, if any, could this help you make? It is equally useful to hear that it would not help. Do not identify your employer or disclose confidential details.

Thank you. The moderator confirms whether any feedback may be quoted and reminds you of the withdrawal code and deletion date. No study conclusion is assumed from taking part.
